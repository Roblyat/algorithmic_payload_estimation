### 1. **Fast Object Inertial Parameter Identification for Collaborative Robots**&#x20;

* **Problem/Issue**: Collaborative robots (cobots) need to identify object inertial parameters (mass, CoM, inertia tensor) quickly. Low SNR in safe cobot regimes makes existing identification slow/inaccurate.
* **SoA**: Classic methods = LS, RTLS, manifold optimization. But they suffer from noise and ill-posedness.
* **Math Background**: Rigid body dynamics, approximation of dynamics, point mass discretization (PMD) to approximate inertia. Uses error metrics invariant to mass/size.
* **Methods**:

  * Approximate dynamics to boost SNR.
  * Discretize shape into point masses for fast plausible inertia estimation.
  * Benchmarked vs OLS, RTLS, GEO.
* **Results**: PMD significantly faster, robust under noise. Loses identifiability of full inertia tensor, but good trade-off. Experiments on modular test objects confirm performance.
* **Contribution**: A method tailored for cobot regime, fast enough for online use.
* **Outlook**: Extend to combine with physical consistency constraints, explore hybrid with learning.

### 2. **The Sum of Its Parts: Visual Part Segmentation for Inertial Parameter Identification of Manipulated Objects**&#x20;

* **Problem/Issue**:
  Collaborative robots must identify object inertial parameters (mass, CoM, inertia tensor) safely. Traditional methods require fast, dynamic motions → unsafe for cobots (ISO safety limits). Need robust parameter ID from *slow or stop-and-go motions*.

* **State of the Art**:

  * LS/RTLS approaches sensitive to low SNR.
  * Constrained optimization ensures physical consistency.
  * Recent geometric approaches (e.g., geodesic-based) still sensitive to noise.
  * Noisy FT sensor data limits accuracy.

* **Mathematical Background**:

  * Assumption: objects consist of homogeneous-density parts.
  * Parameter ID reduces to estimating the *mass of each homogeneous part*.
  * Formulated as convex optimization:

    $$
    \min_m ||Am - b||^2 \quad \text{s.t. } m_j \geq 0
    $$
  * Inertial tensor/CoM computed from tetrahedralized point-cloud shape.

* **Methods**:

  * **Homogeneous Part Segmentation (HPS)** = combine vision (RGB-D) + FT sensing.
  * Surface-based clustering + volumetric tetrahedral segmentation (HTC).
  * Initial clustering accelerates HTC (3× faster).
  * Shape reconstruction from point clouds (ball-pivoting).
  * Dataset of 20 segmented workshop tools (open source).

* **Results**:

  * Outperforms OLS (least squares) and GEO (geodesic optimization) in noisy scenarios.
  * Real cobot demo: hammer balancing using identified inertial parameters.
  * Errors: OLS often inconsistent; GEO stable but dependent on strong prior. HPS achieves better CoM and inertia estimation under noise.

* **Contribution**:

  * First integration of visual segmentation + FT data for cobot-safe inertial parameter ID.
  * New dataset for benchmarking.
  * Open-source code and data.

* **Outlook**:

  * Extend to mixed-integer programming with material priors (density lists).
  * Improve segmentation so parts’ centroids are not coplanar.
  * Handle multi-part articulated objects.

* **SoA**: LS / RLS dominate payload ID; RTLS/quaternion filters exist, usually need sensors (F/T, IMU). Observer-based methods emerging.
* **Math Background**: Momentum observer theory, LS estimation, filtering of kinematics, calibration with virtual object.
* **Methods**:

  * Introduce *virtual calibration object* to improve model online.
* **Results**: Tested on Franka Emika Panda. Novel method reduced error compared to baseline filtering. Calibration step improves robustness to trajectory mismatch.
* **Contribution**: First integration of momentum observer torque estimates into online payload ID. Practical calibration strategy.
* **Outlook**: Broaden to dynamic tasks, extend calibration beyond single trajectories.

### 6. **Accurate Payload Dynamics Estimation and Compensation of a Robotic Manipulator without External Motion Measuring Sensors** (EECAR 2025)

* **Problem/Issue**:
  Estimating **true contact force/torque (F/T)** during interaction is difficult when payloads introduce additional inertial forces. Existing methods often need extra sensors (IMUs, external velocity/acceleration sensors) or accurate CAD models, which are impractical in real deployments.

* **SoA**:

  * **Model-based** payload estimation: uses robot dynamics + torque/current sensors but depends heavily on accurate models.
  * **6D F/T + IMU methods**: estimate inertial parameters, but require additional velocity/acceleration sensing.
  * Existing approaches often rely on **least squares (LS)**, vulnerable to noise and ill-conditioning.
  * Some use **recursive total least squares (RTLS)** or **Kalman filters** for robustness.

* **Math Background**:

  * Newton–Euler dynamics with payload mass, center of mass (CoM), and inertia tensor.
  * Step-by-step **decoupling estimation** (offsets → mass/CoM → inertia tensor).
  * LS regression for inertial parameter estimation.
  * Fourier-series-based **excitation trajectory optimization** to reduce regression ill-conditioning.
  * Second-order filter to estimate payload acceleration from joint kinematics.

* **Methods**:

  * Relies **only** on a wrist-mounted 6D F/T sensor (no IMU, no CAD).
  * Stepwise identification pipeline:

    1. Estimate and compensate **F/T sensor offsets** (gravity alignment procedure).
    2. Identify **mass & CoM** under static poses using LS.
    3. Identify **inertia tensor** under dynamic motions.
  * Designed optimized excitation trajectory using finite Fourier series to maximize parameter observability.
  * Non-contact F/T reconstructed from identified payload dynamics, then subtracted from measured F/T → **true contact force**.

* **Results**:

  * Experiments on 7-DoF ROKAE xMate ER7 Pro with ultrasound probe payload.
  * Payload mass estimated with <10% deviation vs ground truth.
  * Compensation significantly reduced non-contact force artifacts in both linear and rotational motions.
  * Outperformed naïve offset-only or gravity-only compensation, especially in dynamic phases.

* **Contribution**:

  * Novel **online payload estimation & compensation** approach using only wrist F/T sensor + robot kinematics.
  * Removes need for external velocity/acceleration sensors.
  * Provides both **payload inertial parameters** and improved **contact F/T estimation**.

* **Outlook**:

  * Improve acceleration estimation (filtering causes delays/oscillations).
  * Extend to higher payload dynamics accuracy for industrial tasks.
  * Explore recursive or probabilistic identification (RTLS, Bayesian).

* **Focus**:
  **Payload inertial parameter identification** (mass, CoM, inertia tensor) + **contact force compensation**.

### 7. **On the Fully Decoupled Rigid-Body Dynamics Identification of Serial Industrial Robots** (IEEE T-RO, 2025)

* **Problem/Issue**:
  Accurate identification of rigid-body dynamics (friction, link inertia, gravity, payload parameters) in industrial robots is challenging due to strong multi-DoF coupling. Traditional coupled system identification often leads to poor excitation of small-magnitude parameters and inaccurate estimation.

* **SoA**:

  * **Direct measurement (DPM)**: precise but impractical (needs disassembly, expensive equipment).
  * **CAD models**: available but ignore joint dynamics and assembly effects.
  * **System identification (SID)**: practical but limited by coupling and ill-conditioning of regressions.
  * Existing methods: ordinary least squares (OLS), weighted LS, recursive LS, EKF, heuristic optimization; partially decoupled approaches exist but still suffer residual coupling.

* **Math Background**:

  * Rigid-body dynamics: τ = H(q)q̈ + C(q,q̇)q̇ + g(q) + τf(q̇).
  * Linear regression of torques into gravitational, inertial, and frictional components.
  * Harmonic exciting trajectories optimized via condition number minimization.
  * Least-squares estimation; constrained LS for physical consistency.

* **Methods**:

  * Proposed **Fully Decoupled Rigid-Body Dynamics Identification (FDRDI)**.
  * Key innovation: **reciprocating S-curve (RSC)** trajectories to symmetrically cancel couplings.
  * Stepwise decoupling:

    1. Isolate & estimate **frictional parameters**.
    2. Identify **link gravitational** terms (mass centers).
    3. Identify **diagonal inertia** parameters (ZZi, IAi).
    4. Identify **off-diagonal inertia** terms.
    5. Use load/no-load runs to estimate **payload mass, CoM, inertia**.
  * Each parameter set is isolated with independent regression.

* **Results**:

  * Tested on **COMAU RACER3** and **FLEXIV RIZON4** robots.
  * Achieved significantly lower RMS torque prediction errors than CRDI (coupled) and PDRDI (partial decoupling).
  * Identified payload mass and CoM with high accuracy (< few % deviation).
  * Frictional and inertial parameters captured across full velocity/acceleration ranges.
  * Outperformed CLS and iterative hybrid LS approaches, showing decoupling > advanced estimation in importance.

* **Contribution**:

  * First **fully decoupled** SID method for industrial robots.
  * Ensures sufficient excitation for each parameter.
  * Achieves accurate identification of both robot body and payload parameters.
  * Robust against friction variations with different payloads.
  * Provides validated improvements over coupled and partially decoupled approaches.

* **Outlook**:

  * Extend to more complex friction models and flexible-joint dynamics.
  * Apply to online/adaptive identification for changing payloads.
  * Combine with probabilistic approaches (e.g., Bayesian filters) for robustness.

* **Focus**:
  **Rigid-body dynamics & payload parameter identification** via **fully decoupled system identification**.

### 8. **A Two-Stage Payload Dynamic Parameter Identification Method for Interactive Industrial Robots With Large Components** (T-ASE 2025)

* **Problem/Issue**:
  Accurate payload identification in industrial robots handling **large, heavy components** is hard. Existing methods often neglect certain error sources (sensor drift, base inclination, inertial forces) or lack robustness for online operation.

* **SoA**:

  * **Model-based** LS, WLS, DWLS approaches → simple but noise-sensitive.
  * **Recursive total least squares (RTLS)** and Kalman-based methods → more robust but costly or incomplete.
  * Previous works mostly validated on **light payloads (<5 kg)**, not suitable for high-payload collaborative assembly.

* **Math Background**:

  * Newton–Euler dynamics equations with payload mass, CoM, inertia tensor.
  * Total least squares (TLS) vs. recursive restricted TLS (RRTLS).
  * Fourier-series-based excitation trajectories for observability optimization.

* **Methods**:

  * **Two-stage identification**:

    1. **Static ID** → estimate base inclination, sensor drift, mass, CoM.
    2. **Dynamic ID** → inertia tensor using RRTLS with optimized excitation trajectory.
  * Introduced **feasible workspace planning** for safe robot poses.
  * Compensation applied to improve **contact force sensing**.

* **Results**:

  * Tested on KUKA KR60HA with large payload (\~35 kg).
  * Outperformed LS and RTLS in parameter accuracy and robustness.
  * Improved **contact force sensing** under high/low speed trajectories.
  * Max residual compensated error \~3 N force, \~1.7 Nm torque.

* **Contribution**:

  * First **comprehensive ID framework** considering multiple error sources for large industrial payloads.
  * **Online-capable, low-cost RRTLS** for dynamic identification.
  * Validated in **human-robot collaborative assembly** with real aircraft wing components.

* **Outlook**:

  * Further reduce residual errors with advanced filtering.
  * Extend to **recursive Bayesian ID** for uncertainty handling.
  * Broaden to multi-payload or unstructured assembly scenarios.

* **Focus**:
  **Payload inertial parameter ID** (mass, CoM, inertia) with **contact force compensation** for large industrial components.

### 9. **Identifying Current Dynamics of Robot Payload Based on Iterative Weighting Estimation** (IEEE TIM, 2025)

* **Problem/Issue**:
  Traditional payload identification methods rely on **joint torque signals**. But most industrial robots lack torque sensors, and torque estimates from current × torque constant suffer from \~10% uncertainty, which propagates into large **cumulative identification errors**.

* **SoA**:

  * Classical methods: LS, WLS, and maximum-likelihood using torque signals.
  * Differential approaches: comparing trajectories with/without payload.
  * Improvements: double weighting, nonlinear optimization, Kalman filtering.
  * Data-driven: neural methods, but heavy on data/computation.
  * Still limited by dependence on torque signals & friction modeling errors.

* **Math Background**:

  * Dynamics: Newton–Euler equations with current–torque linear relation.
  * Current dynamics model: $i = H \chi$, with regressor $H$, parameters $\chi$.
  * Iterative weighting → **WLS** solution with outlier rejection.
  * Improved **Stribeck friction model** (arctangent + nonlinear viscous term).

* **Methods**:

  * Novel **current-based identification model** (no torque constants).
  * Iterative weighting scheme normalizes regressors, rejects outliers, solves WLS.
  * Improved friction model embedded in identification.
  * Tested on **UR10 robot**, using optimized Fourier excitation trajectories.
  * Compared against 4 torque-based payload ID methods.

* **Results**:

  * Identified **current dynamics** (56 parameters) for robot & payload.
  * Significantly lower RMSE vs torque-based methods across 3 payloads (3.5–4 kg).
  * More robust to noise/outliers due to weighting.
  * Practical demo: improved **collision detection** when payload dynamics compensated.

* **Contribution**:

  * First work to propose **payload identification via current dynamics** (no torque sensors/constants).
  * Iterative weighting estimation improves robustness and accuracy.
  * Shows application to collision detection & potential trajectory control.

* **Outlook**:

  * Investigate **physical interpretability** of current-dynamics parameters.
  * Extend to trajectory tracking control with current feedforward.
  * Explore broader application in industrial HRC (human–robot collaboration).

* **Focus**:
  **Payload parameter identification** via **current dynamics + iterative weighting**.

### 10 **An Accurate Identification Method Based on Double Weighting for Inertial Parameters of Robot Payloads** (Robotica 2022)

* **Problem/Issue**:
  Payload inertial parameter identification is critical for robot dynamics and control. Existing methods (OLS, WLS, RLS, Kalman filters) struggle with **outliers, noise, and friction effects**, reducing accuracy.

* **SoA**:

  * **OLS/WLS-based ID**: often inaccurate, sensitive to noise.
  * **Force/torque sensor approaches**: accurate but not widely available.
  * **Differential torque methods**: estimate by comparing with/without payload but suffer trajectory mismatch errors.
  * **Online methods (RLS, KF, adaptive)**: faster but less accurate due to reduced data usage.
  * Weighting techniques exist in robot dynamics ID but had **not been applied to payload estimation**.

* **Math Background**:

  * Inverse dynamics model with payload mass, CoM, inertia tensor.
  * Nonlinear friction model for actuators.
  * Weighted Least Squares (WLS).
  * **Double weighting technique**: covariance-based normalization + data weighting for outlier suppression.

* **Methods**:

  1. Modified dynamic parameter identification using **two-loop weighting update** (covariance + data weighting).
  2. Construct **payload identification model** with double weighting.
  3. Include **variations of linear & nonlinear friction parameters** caused by payload.
  4. Solve via WLS after applying both weightings.
  5. Experiments on UR10 robot with Fourier-series-based excitation trajectories.

* **Results**:

  * Outperformed four baselines (OLS, WLS, differential torque, coefficient-based).
  * Achieved **highest accuracy in mass and CoM estimation**.
  * Relative error in mass reduced by up to **10×** compared to WLS, **43×** compared to differential method.
  * Inertia tensor estimation improved but remained challenging.
  * Double weighting significantly suppressed outliers and improved robustness.

* **Contribution**:

  * **First application of double weighting** in payload parameter ID.
  * Incorporates friction variation modeling.
  * Improves robustness and accuracy of offline payload ID for industrial robots.

* **Outlook**:

  * Extend method for **real-time online payload estimation**.
  * Improve accuracy of inertia tensor estimation.
  * Apply to broader industrial settings.

* **Focus**:
  **Offline payload inertial parameter identification** (mass, CoM, inertia tensor) with **double weighting and friction modeling**.

### 11. **Payload Identification and Gravity/Inertial Compensation for Six-Dimensional Force/Torque Sensor with a Fast and Robust Trajectory Design Approach** (Sensors, 2022)

* **Problem/Issue**:
  Force/Torque (F/T) sensors are widely used in manipulators, but their measurements are corrupted by **payload-induced gravity and inertial forces**. Without proper compensation, accurate force estimation and control are not possible. Existing compensation methods often rely on slow or poorly conditioned identification trajectories.

* **SoA**:

  * Classical approaches: Least Squares (LS), Recursive LS (RLS), and Extended Kalman Filters (EKF).
  * Issues: trajectory design often leads to ill-conditioned regression → poor robustness.
  * Payload inertial parameter estimation is often computationally heavy or sensitive to noise.

* **Math Background**:

  * Newton–Euler dynamics of manipulator with payload mass, CoM, and inertia tensor.
  * Inertial parameter identification as a regression problem: τ = Y(q, q̇, q̈)θ.
  * Conditioning analysis of regression matrix.

* **Methods**:

  * Proposed **fast and robust trajectory design method** for inertial parameter identification.
  * Optimized excitation trajectories to minimize regression ill-conditioning.
  * Online compensation of **gravity and inertial loads** on the 6D F/T sensor.
  * Validated with both simulations and physical robot experiments.

* **Results**:

  * Achieved **fast convergence** of payload parameter estimates.
  * Compensation significantly improved **accuracy of F/T measurements**, removing bias from payload dynamics.
  * Outperformed standard LS and poorly designed excitation trajectories in robustness tests.

* **Contribution**:

  * First systematic **trajectory optimization method** for payload ID in 6D F/T sensors.
  * Improved accuracy and robustness of gravity/inertia compensation.
  * Practical approach for real robot tasks requiring reliable F/T sensing.

* **Outlook**:

  * Extend method to **high-DoF manipulators** with varying payloads.
  * Combine trajectory optimization with **real-time adaptive filters** for on-the-fly compensation.
  * Potential application in **human-robot interaction and assembly tasks**.

* **Focus**:
  **Payload inertial parameter identification** (mass, CoM, inertia tensor) for **F/T sensor compensation**.

### 14. [old but robust] **Dynamic Model Identification for Industrial Robots** (Swevers, Verdonck & De Schutter, IEEE CSM 2007)

* **Category**:
  Q1 — **Classical / Observers**
  (Uses rigid-body dynamics, maximum likelihood / weighted least squares estimation, and periodic excitation for parameter identification.)

* **Problem/Issue**:
  Industrial robots need accurate dynamic models for **offline programming**, **task optimization**, and **advanced model-based control**. Standard models are inaccurate due to missing link inertial data, friction, and actuator dynamics.

* **Math Background**:

  * **Rigid-body dynamics** (Newton–Euler / Lagrangian):

    $$
    \tau = M(q)\ddot{q} + C(q,\dot{q}) + g(q)
    $$
  * Reformulated as **linear-in-parameters**:

    $$
    \tau = \Phi(q, \dot{q}, \ddot{q})\theta
    $$

    where $\theta$ are barycentric parameters (link inertias, masses, etc.).
  * Friction modeled with **Coulomb + viscous** terms.

* **Methods**:

  * **Periodic bandlimited excitation** for experiment design → simplifies signal processing and parameter estimation.
  * **Fourier-based differentiation** → noise-free velocity/acceleration estimation.
  * **Weighted Least Squares (WLS) / Maximum Likelihood** parameter estimation.
  * **Model validation** via torque prediction accuracy.

* **Results**:

  * Experiments on **KUKA IR 361** and **KR 15** robots.
  * Accurate identification of link inertial and friction parameters.
  * **Payload identification plug-in** demonstrated (estimation of mass within 5%, CoM within 1 cm).
  * RMS torque prediction errors comparable to noise level (~few Nm).

* **Contribution**:

  * Established **periodic excitation-based integrated framework** (experiment design → signal processing → parameter estimation → validation).
  * Demonstrated **industrial relevance** with payload identification modules for KUKA robots.
  * Influential review + methodology paper in **dynamic model identification** for industrial robots.

### 15. **A Novel Sliding Mode Momentum Observer for Collaborative Robot Collision Detection**

(Long, Dang, Sun, Wang & Gui, *Machines* 2022)

* **Category**:
  Q1 — **Classical / Observers**
  (Observer-based, sliding mode + momentum observer, used for collision detection)

* **Problem/Issue**:
  Collision detection in **collaborative robots** without external sensors suffers from a trade-off between **sensitivity, smoothness, and noise robustness**. Existing momentum observers have limitations: noise immunity vs. response speed.

* **Math Background**:

  * Rigid-body dynamics with external disturbance torque
  * Generalized momentum formulation:
    [
    p = M(q)\dot{q}, \quad \dot{p} = \tau + C(q,\dot{q})\dot{q} - g(q) + \tau_\text{ext} - \rho
    ]
  * Sliding-mode reaching law + stability proof.

* **Methods**:

  * **NSOMO** (Novel Sliding Mode Momentum Observer) with a **new reaching law**.
  * **TVDT** (Time-Varying Dynamic Threshold) model learned offline from data to discriminate collision torque from disturbance.
  * Hardware validation on Franka Emika Panda.

* **Results**:

  * Higher bandwidth and better noise immunity than GM/SOMO.
  * Reliable detection of **dynamic and quasistatic collisions**.
  * Collision location assessment possible.

* **Contribution**:

  * Introduced **sliding-mode momentum observer + adaptive threshold** framework.
  * Improves **safety in physical human–robot interaction** without requiring external sensors.

## 🔎 Conclusion of Q1 Research (Classical / Observers)

### **Scope Covered**

* Q1 research spans **force/torque estimation**, **external disturbance estimation**, and **inertial/payload identification**.
* Methods build on *classical observers*: Momentum Observer (MO), Disturbance Observer (DOB), Kalman filters, Sliding Mode Observers, Least Squares.
* The papers adapt these methods to address **noise, robustness, and safety constraints** in collaborative robot settings.

---

### **What They Estimate**

* **Internal robot parameters** (state & torque observers):

  * *Contact Force and Torque Sensing (Kalman filter)* → estimates **joint torques** and **contact forces** without external sensors.
  * *External Torque Estimation (sliding mode)* → focuses on **external torques acting on the manipulator**, accounting for friction and model errors.

* **Payload / Object parameters**:

  * *Fast Object Inertial Parameter ID (PMD)* → identifies **payload mass, CoM, inertia tensor** (object parameters).
  * *Online Payload ID using Momentum Observer* → estimates **payload mass and CoM online** during tasks.
  * *The Sum of Its Parts (Visual Part Segmentation)* → estimates **payload inertial parameters** using visual segmentation + FT sensing.

* **Mixed focus (both robot & payload)**:

  * Momentum observer–based methods estimate **joint torques** (internal) and use them for **payload ID** (external).
  * Kalman filter methods combine **internal torque estimation** with **external contact force estimation**.

---

### **Mathematical Backbone**

* **Observers**: Momentum Observer, Disturbance Observer, Sliding Mode Observer, Kalman Filter.
* **Identification tools**: Least Squares, Recursive LS, Convex Optimization, Geometric Constraints.
* **Noise robustness**: Adaptive filtering, point-mass discretization, part segmentation with priors.

---

### **State of the Art Trend**

* Shift away from purely **internal robot dynamics estimation** → toward **payload/object parameter identification**.
* Classical observer tools remain central but are extended with:

  * **Robustness enhancements** (adaptive filters, higher-order observers).
  * **Safety adaptations** (methods that work at cobot speeds).
  * **Sensor/data fusion** (vision + force sensors, calibration tricks).

---

### **Overall Coverage**

* **Robot Internal Estimation**: Joint torques, external torques, contact forces.
* **Payload Estimation**: Mass, CoM, inertia tensor, parameters of manipulated objects.
* **Both**: Some methods explicitly link robot torque observers → payload estimation.

---

✅ **Conclusion:**
Q1 (Classical / Observers) research covers **both robot and payload estimation tasks**. The traditional observer frameworks remain powerful but are evolving:

* For **internal estimation**: torque/force observers improve contact awareness without sensors.
* For **payload/object estimation**: inertial parameter ID methods (PMD, visual segmentation, MO-based) push toward cobot-safe, online identification of manipulated objects.
* The frontier is in **hybrid estimation**: using internal observers to feed into external payload ID, especially under noisy or constrained conditions.
